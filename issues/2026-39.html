This Week in AI — Week of September 21, 2026

ISO Week 2026-39 · Distributed to: Secure Digital Core, Accenture Security Prepared by: Jacob Nosal | jacob.nosal@accenture.com

At a Glance

The week was defined by a same-day frontier launch and price war on September 22: Anthropic shipped Claude Opus 5.5, and roughly 90 minutes later OpenAI countered with two cheaper GPT-6 models, Sol and Luna (SiliconANGLE). OpenAI undercut aggressively — GPT-6 Sol lands at half the price of its predecessor and matches Opus 5.5’s capability tier at roughly half Anthropic’s per-token cost, while GPT-6 Luna drops to $0.10/$0.50. Notably, neither lab benchmarked the other’s new model, so the headline claims are all vendor-reported and independent evaluation is still pending. For enterprise AI programs the signal is cost: capable agents are getting materially cheaper to run, which lowers the barrier to large-scale agent deployment — and correspondingly enlarges the prompt-injection blast radius that SDC client conversations should already be addressing.

Model Releases & Benchmarks

Anthropic — Claude Opus 5.5 (September 22) First model in the new 5.5 family. Anthropic reports a ~20% price cut to $4 / $20 per million tokens (input/output), cache reads at $0.20/MTok, and claims roughly 40% lower cost on typical workloads versus Opus 5 with over 30% faster output. Available on AWS, Google Cloud, and Azure; Anthropic signaled Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 “within weeks.” Vendor-reported benchmarks include Terminal-Bench 4.0 66.4%, CursorBench 4.0 57.8%, and AutomationBench 40.0%. (Anthropic · DataCamp)

💡 Why it matters — A flagship refresh that cuts price while claiming capability gains keeps downward pressure on agentic-workload economics; treat the benchmark deltas as vendor-reported until third parties confirm.

OpenAI — GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Luna (September 22) Released about 90 minutes after Opus 5.5 as faster, cheaper distillations of GPT-6 Astra (Astra shipped September 3 as a limited preview). OpenAI cut token prices in half: Sol at $2 / $10 per million tokens (down from $4/$20) and Luna at $0.10 / $0.50 (down from $0.20/$1.20). Sol’s pricing matches Opus 5.5’s tier at roughly half the cost. OpenAI’s vendor-reported figures include Sol at 56.4% and Luna at 66.6% on its “Agents’ Last Exam” at max effort, with claimed per-task cost reductions of 60–96% versus prior Claude models. (OpenAI · TechCrunch · VentureBeat)

💡 Why it matters — Luna’s $0.10/$0.50 pricing makes high-volume agentic pipelines dramatically cheaper to operate; the cost curve, not the benchmark bar, is the more durable enterprise signal this week.

Benchmark caveat — no head-to-head. Neither vendor evaluated the other’s new model in its launch materials; the only overlapping metric is AutomationBench (Opus 5.5 40.0% vs GPT-6 Sol 33.2%, both vendor-reported). (The Neuron)

💡 Why it matters — Procurement and architecture decisions should wait on neutral third-party benchmarks (SWE-bench, OSWorld, Terminal-Bench) rather than launch-day charts.

Safety & Research

arXiv — “SkillSecurer: Detecting and Patching Prompt-Injection Vulnerabilities in AI Agent Skills” (preprint, September 15) A framework to statically detect and automatically patch prompt-injection vulnerabilities inside the reusable “skills” that agentic systems load. Arrives as agent skill/plugin ecosystems expand across enterprise deployments. (arXiv:2609.14079)

💡 Why it matters — As agents assemble behavior from third-party skills, per-skill injection scanning becomes an AI supply-chain control, not just an application-layer one — a concrete technique to fold into agentic-AI assurance.

Enterprise & Security Implications

Cheaper, more capable agents enlarge the prompt-injection attack surface. Prompt injection — especially indirect injection via untrusted content the agent ingests — remains the dominant cause of agentic-AI security failures in production, with documented findings against widely used enterprise tools including Slack AI, Microsoft 365 Copilot, Cursor, and GitHub’s MCP integrations. The operative risk model is the “lethal trifecta”: untrusted input + access to private data + an exfiltration channel. This week’s launches (GPT-6 Luna at $0.10/$0.50) make it economically trivial to deploy far more agents, scaling that exposure. (Help Net Security / OWASP · Airia)

🛡️ Why it matters for SDC — Client talking points: (1) normalize and segregate untrusted content before it reaches the model; (2) enforce least-privilege on agent tool authorities so a hijacked agent cannot reach private data and an exfiltration path simultaneously; (3) require runtime visibility over agent actions; (4) treat third-party agent skills/plugins as a software supply chain subject to injection scanning (see the SkillSecurer research above).
