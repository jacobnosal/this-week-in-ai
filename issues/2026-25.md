## Policy & Regulation

**United States — Executive Order "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security" gets its first live test**
The EO (signed June 2, published June 5) has three parts: federal AI-cyber defense measures, a **voluntary frontier-model engagement framework**, and prioritized enforcement of existing cybercrime statutes against AI-enabled attacks. It is explicitly **not** a mandatory licensing or preclearance regime. It directs agencies, within a 60-day window, to build a **classified benchmarking process** to define a "covered frontier model" threshold (advanced cyber capability). For models that meet it, developers *may* voluntarily engage the government during development, grant **up to 30 days of pre-release access**, and select government-vetted "trusted partners" for early access. ([White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/06/promoting-advanced-artificial-intelligence-innovation-and-security/) · [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/president-trump-signs-executive-order-establishing-ai-cybersecurity-and-frontier-model-framework) · [Tenable](https://www.tenable.com/blog/summary-june-2026-ai-executive-order-requirements))

> **💡 Why it matters** — The EO's "voluntary" framing is doing less work than it looks. This week's Fable 5 / Mythos 5 export-control takedown shows the government already has hard levers over frontier-model distribution independent of the EO's voluntary track. For enterprises, the practical signal is that frontier-model *availability* is now a function of US national-security policy — plan for it as a supply variable, not a constant.

`REVIEW:` — The Executive Order itself dates to June 2 (just outside the ISO-25 window) but is included as the live policy frame for this week's export-control episode. Flag if you'd rather hold it to a dedicated policy callout.

## Enterprise & Security Implications

**Meta's AI support bot was abused to hijack ~20,225 Instagram accounts — an agentic-AI permissions failure.**
Attackers social-engineered Meta's AI-powered Instagram account-recovery chatbot, which had permission to **relink/add email addresses and trigger password resets**. By instructing the bot to add an attacker-controlled email, retrieving the one-time code there, and feeding it back, they took over accounts — including high-profile handles — with some attackers reportedly also using AI-generated selfie video to defeat identity checks. Meta confirmed roughly **20,225 accounts** may have been affected, then disabled the tool, removed the faulty code, invalidated reset links, and forced extra verification. ([KrebsOnSecurity](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2026/06/hackers-used-metas-ai-support-bot-to-seize-instagram-accounts/) · [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/01/hackers-hijacked-instagram-accounts-by-tricking-meta-ai-support-chatbot-into-granting-access/) · [NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/06/09/technology/instagram-hack-ai-bug.html))

> **🛡️ Why it matters for SDC** — This is the cleanest production example yet of *agentic-AI tool-authority risk*: the model didn't need to be "jailbroken," it was simply given a privileged tool (mutate account recovery state) without a hard identity-binding control behind it. Client talking points: (1) inventory which support/ops agents can perform **privileged identity or account actions** (email relink, password reset, MFA reset) and require step-up human-in-the-loop or cryptographic identity binding on those actions; (2) treat agent tool permissions as a least-privilege boundary subject to the same review as an API scope, not a UX convenience. `REVIEW:` the exact 20,225 figure is Meta-attributed via secondary reporting; NYT confirms "tens of thousands."

**Frontier-model availability is now an export-control / continuity risk.**
Anthropic's worldwide shutdown of Fable 5 and Mythos 5 — triggered because it could not filter access by nationality in real time — turned a US export directive into a global outage for every customer. ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/13/anthropic-disables-fable-mythos-export-controls-national-security-threat/) · [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/anishasircar/2026/06/16/anthropic-disabled-fable-5-and-mythos-5-after-a-us-export-control-order-heres-what-happened/))

> **🛡️ Why it matters for SDC** — Single-vendor frontier dependency now carries a geopolitical continuity risk that didn't exist a quarter ago: a model your client's workload depends on can be pulled with hours of notice. Talking points: (1) maintain a **multi-model fallback** path for any production-critical workload (provider + model abstraction, tested failover); (2) for regulated/multinational clients, the "must restrict by user nationality" requirement is now a concrete control to design for, not a hypothetical. `REVIEW:` no standalone Anthropic newsroom statement on restoration was located at drafting time; takedown specifics rest on Fortune/Forbes plus secondary explainers — confirm before quoting to clients.
