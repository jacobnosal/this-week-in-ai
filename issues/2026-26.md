## Safety & Research

*Alignment papers, interpretability findings, red-team disclosures, model evaluations, governance frameworks.*

**OWASP — *State of Agentic AI Security and Governance* v2.01 (June 2026)**
The 2026 edition shifts from theoretical risk to **evidence-based**, cataloguing CVEs, vendor advisories, and breach reports across nearly every agentic-risk category. Prompt injection maps to **6 of OWASP's 10** agentic top risks; the report codifies the **"Lethal Trifecta"** (private-data access + untrusted-content exposure + external communication = exfiltration-by-design) and Meta's **"Agents Rule of Two"** (an autonomous agent should satisfy at most two of those three without human approval). It notes only **37%** of organizations have shadow-AI detection, and three supply-chain layers (malicious MCP servers, agent execution environments, package repos — e.g. the LiteLLM PyPI backdoor that hit CrewAI, DSPy, and MS GraphRAG). ([Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/06/11/owasp-prompt-injection-ai-security-failures/))

> **🛡️ Why it matters for SDC** — This is a ready-made control framework for client conversations. The "Lethal Trifecta" and "Rule of Two" are concrete, defensible design tests you can apply in an agentic-AI architecture review today — and a live exploit of exactly this pattern landed the same week (see Agentjacking below).

## Policy & Regulation

*US federal/state AI action, EU AI Act developments, NIST guidance, international governance.*

**United States — Foreign-national access to a commercial frontier model cut by directive (~June 12)**
The directive behind the Fable 5 / Mythos 5 suspension invoked national-security authorities (reported as a Commerce action) and required Anthropic to block access by **any foreign national, inside or outside the US**, including its own foreign-born staff. The stated basis: a suspected China-linked group accessed the new model, and Trump AI adviser **David Sacks** cited a potential jailbreak (Amazon researchers reportedly flagged vulnerabilities). **Anthropic disputes the basis**, saying it received only *"verbal evidence of a potential narrow, non-universal jailbreak"* and that this should not justify recalling a model deployed to hundreds of millions. ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/17/anthropic-ai-regulation-trump.html) · [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/6/14/us-asks-anthropic-to-block-global-access-to-top-ai-models-why-it-matters))

> **🛡️ Why it matters for SDC** — `REVIEW:` This is the first time foreign-national access to a *commercial* US frontier model has been cut by government order — a precedent distinct from chip/export controls. Flag two client-facing implications: (1) **personnel** — "US-persons-only" access restrictions could attach to specific models, a real constraint for global delivery teams and foreign-national engineers; (2) **continuity** — government action is now a documented reason a model can disappear. Both belong in AI-vendor risk registers.

**Clarification — this is NOT the June 2 Executive Order.** Executive Order 14409, *"Promoting Advanced AI Innovation and Security"* (signed **June 2, 2026**), establishes a **voluntary** covered-frontier-model process (optional pre-release government access of up to 30 days), directs CISA/cyber upgrades, and prioritizes prosecution of AI-enabled intrusions — while **explicitly disclaiming** mandatory licensing, preclearance, or permitting, and containing **no** export-control or foreign-national-access provisions. The Fable 5 / Mythos 5 action is a **separate** directive. ([White House — EO 14409](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/06/promoting-advanced-artificial-intelligence-innovation-and-security/))

> **🎯 Why it matters for Accenture** — Expect the two to be conflated in client briefings. The defensible read: the EO is a voluntary cooperation framework; the model suspension is a separate national-security action. Keeping them distinct avoids overstating what US law currently *mandates* of AI developers.

The final General-Purpose AI Code of Practice has now been signed by roughly two dozen providers — including **Anthropic, Google, IBM, Microsoft, Mistral, OpenAI, and Cohere**. **Meta did not sign**, and **xAI signed only the Safety & Security chapter.** Commission enforcement powers under the Act activate **August 2, 2026**. ([Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/eu-ai-act-gpai-model-obligations-in-force-and-final-gpai-code-of-practice-in-place) · [EU AI Act](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/introduction-to-code-of-practice/))

> **🎯 Why it matters for Accenture** — Code-of-Practice signature status is now a usable due-diligence signal when advising on model selection for EU-facing systems: signatories have committed to a transparency/copyright/safety baseline; non- and partial-signatories (Meta, xAI) have not. ## Enterprise & Security Implications

*Developments with direct relevance to client conversations: agentic AI risk, prompt injection, model supply chain, governance.*

**"Agentjacking" — a Sentry error report can hijack Claude Code, Cursor, and Codex**
Researchers at **Tenet Security** disclosed **Agentjacking**: an attacker who knows a target's public **Sentry DSN** submits a crafted error report containing **markdown injection**; when the **Sentry MCP server** returns that event to an AI coding agent (**Claude Code, Cursor, OpenAI Codex**), it is rendered as *trusted system output*, and the agent can be steered into executing attacker-controlled code on the developer's machine — **no phishing, malware, or infrastructure breach required**. Using only public Sentry APIs, researchers found **2,388 exposed organizations** (Fortune 100 down to independent developers) and observed 100+ agents acting on injected errors in testing. Sentry acknowledged the disclosure on **June 3** and shipped a filter blocking the specific proof-of-concept string — but **not the underlying architectural path** (any MCP source that relays attacker-controllable content to an agent as trusted output). ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/06/agentjacking-attack-tricks-ai-coding.html) · [Tenet Security](https://tenetsecurity.ai/blog/agentjacking-coding-agents-with-fake-sentry-errors/) · [SC Media](https://www.scworld.com/brief/agentjacking-attack-exploits-ai-coding-tools-with-fake-error-reports))

> **🛡️ Why it matters for SDC** — This is the indirect-prompt-injection-via-tooling pattern we've tracked (Semantic Kernel last month), now hitting the **AI coding agents teams actually run**. Concrete client actions: (1) **treat every MCP server as an untrusted-input boundary** — data returned by an MCP tool (error reports, tickets, logs, web content) must not be handled as trusted instructions; (2) **require human-in-the-loop confirmation** before an agent executes shell/code derived from external content; (3) for Sentry specifically, note the vendor fix is **partial** — the architectural exposure remains, so compensating controls (least-privilege agent permissions, sandboxed execution) are still required. Make "what untrusted content can reach this agent as 'system output'?" a standard agentic-security review question.

> **🛡️ Why it matters for SDC (framework angle)** — Pair the Agentjacking exploit with the OWASP v2.01 framework above: the attack is a textbook **Lethal Trifecta** instance (the agent has code-execution capability + ingests untrusted Sentry content + can act externally). Clients can use the "Rule of Two" as the remediation rule of thumb — break one leg of the trifecta (e.g., remove autonomous execution, or gate external content) and the exploit class collapses.
