## Safety & Research

**[9to5Google — Report: staff doubt unreleased frontier models can be "safely" released (Nov 24)]**
A report relayed Nov 24, citing more than two dozen OpenAI and Anthropic staff, claims employees believe their *unreleased* next-generation frontier models may not be safely releasable or fully controllable. Anthropic's reported response was that keeping a model internal does not, by itself, make it safe. `REVIEW:` this rests on anonymous staff accounts relayed through secondary coverage — treat as a reported claim for tracking, not as a verified disclosure. ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/11/24/openai-anthropic-new-models-safety-concerns-report))

> **💡 Why it matters** — Whether or not the specifics hold, "controllability of the next model" is becoming a board-level narrative that will feed into both regulation and client risk questions. Expect to be asked "is the model your vendor is selling us actually controllable?" — have a framework answer ready (evals, deployment gating, human-in-the-loop authorities).

**[arXiv — "Scaling Adaptive Prompt Injection Attacks with Agent Swarms" (November)]**
A November preprint describes using coordinated "swarms" of agents to adaptively scale prompt-injection attacks against other agentic systems — automating the search for a working injection rather than hand-crafting one. ([arXiv:2611.11861](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11861))

> **💡 Why it matters** — Automated, adaptive injection-finding shortens the gap between "our guardrail passed a static test" and "an attacker found the one phrasing that defeats it." Point-in-time red-team results age quickly; argue for continuous adversarial testing of agent deployments, not one-off sign-off.

## Policy & Regulation

**[EU — AI Act Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744): Article 50(2) watermarking deadline Dec 2, 2026]**
The Digital Omnibus pushed the heaviest *high-risk* obligations back (Annex III standalone systems to Dec 2, 2027; product-embedded to Aug 2, 2028) but left the Article 50 **transparency** duties — chatbot-interaction disclosure and labeling of synthetic media/deepfakes — in force from Aug 2, 2026. A new Article 111(4) gives providers of generative systems placed on the market **before** Aug 2, 2026 a grace period to comply with Article 50(2) synthetic-content marking that **ends December 2, 2026**. ([Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/) · [Cloud Security Alliance](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-omnibus-vii-deadline-delay-20260/) · [ActuIA](https://www.actuia.com/en/news/ai-act-obligations-in-force-and-delays-under-omnibus-2026-1744/))

> **💡 Why it matters** — This updates the May watermarking callout with a hard date that is now days out. Clients who shipped generative features in the EU before Aug 2026 and assumed the Omnibus bought them runway are mistaken on this point: synthetic-content marking obligations bite Dec 2. `REVIEW:` sources differ on the exact grace-period length (three vs four months) — confirm the deadline against Article 111(4) of the Official Journal text before advising a client.

## Enterprise & Security Implications

**[Agentic AI credential boundaries — AWS Bedrock AgentCore "AgentCorruption" (Zenity Labs; AWS disputes framing)]**
The defining agentic-cloud-security case study continues to drive coverage. Zenity Labs documented a prompt-injection chain in which a public-facing Bedrock AgentCore agent with web-request tooling could be induced to query the instance metadata endpoint (169.254.169.254) and retrieve credentials tied to its execution role; the root causes cited are legacy **IMDSv1** and an **overly broad default execution role**, and the stolen permissions reportedly reached other agents in the same account and region (private chats, source code, long-term memories, and Secrets Manager secrets). AWS disputes the "vulnerability" framing — stating it describes expected/documented behavior, that no cross-account boundary was crossed, and that it is fully mitigated (IMDSv2 by Feb 2026; the over-permissioned-role issue resolved by ~end of Sept 2026). Unit 42 separately demonstrated (Sep 18) an indirect-injection path driving an AgentCore Harness to run as root and read runtime-memory credentials. ([SC World](https://www.scworld.com/brief/amazon-bedrock-agent-vulnerability-allowed-credential-theft-and-code-access) · [The Decoder / Zenity](https://the-decoder.com/a-single-prompt-was-enough-to-hijack-every-ai-agent-in-an-aws-account-zenity-researchers-found/))

> **🛡️ Why it matters for SDC** — Client talking point: agent identity is the new blast radius. Concrete checks to recommend on any Bedrock AgentCore (or equivalent) deployment — (1) enforce **IMDSv2** and block agent tool-use from reaching the metadata endpoint; (2) scope each agent's execution role to **least privilege**, never a broad shared role spanning the account/region; (3) treat an agent's standing credentials as reachable by anyone who can influence its prompt, and segment memory/secrets accordingly. This is less a single CVE than a design pattern clients are repeating.

**[Memory poisoning in agentic systems — vendor research (November)]**
Vendor write-ups cite November research (attributed to Lakera AI) in which poisoned data sources corrupted an agent's **long-term memory**, causing the agent to act on planted instructions in later, unrelated sessions. The primary report was not independently located; treat as vendor-reported. ([Stellar Cyber](https://stellarcyber.ai/learn/agentic-ai-securiry-threats/))

> **🛡️ Why it matters for SDC** — Memory and RAG stores are an injection surface that persists across sessions, unlike a single-turn prompt. Recommend ingestion-time sanitization at the loader/memory-write layer and periodic review of what an agent has "remembered," not just what it was told in the current turn.
