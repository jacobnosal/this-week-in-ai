At a Glance

The week’s defining AI story was regulatory, not a launch. Anthropic’s newest frontier models — Fable 5 and Mythos 5 (released June 9) — stayed dark worldwide through the week after a June 12 U.S. export-control directive barred their distribution to any foreign national; unable to filter access by nationality in real time, Anthropic disabled them for everyone, with no confirmed restoration as of June 19 (Fortune · Forbes). The episode is the first live stress-test of the June 2 Executive Order’s frontier-model security framework. No new frontier flagship shipped — Gemini 3.5 Pro’s GA slipped again and xAI’s Grok V9 missed its mid-June window. The standout enterprise-security lesson was the Meta AI support-bot abuse that hijacked roughly 20,225 Instagram accounts — an agentic-AI tool-permission failure, not a prompt-injection edge case.

Policy & Regulation

United States — Executive Order “Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security” gets its first live test The EO (signed June 2, published June 5) has three parts: federal AI-cyber defense measures, a voluntary frontier-model engagement framework, and prioritized enforcement of existing cybercrime statutes against AI-enabled attacks. It is explicitly not a mandatory licensing or preclearance regime. It directs agencies, within a 60-day window, to build a classified benchmarking process to define a “covered frontier model” threshold (advanced cyber capability). For models that meet it, developers may voluntarily engage the government during development, grant up to 30 days of pre-release access, and select government-vetted “trusted partners” for early access. (White House · Latham & Watkins · Tenable)

💡 Why it matters — The EO’s “voluntary” framing is doing less work than it looks. This week’s Fable 5 / Mythos 5 export-control takedown shows the government already has hard levers over frontier-model distribution independent of the EO’s voluntary track. For enterprises, the practical signal is that frontier-model availability is now a function of US national-security policy — plan for it as a supply variable, not a constant.

REVIEW: — The Executive Order itself dates to June 2 (just outside the ISO-25 window) but is included as the live policy frame for this week’s export-control episode. Flag if you’d rather hold it to a dedicated policy callout.

Enterprise & Security Implications

Meta’s AI support bot was abused to hijack ~20,225 Instagram accounts — an agentic-AI permissions failure. Attackers social-engineered Meta’s AI-powered Instagram account-recovery chatbot, which had permission to relink/add email addresses and trigger password resets. By instructing the bot to add an attacker-controlled email, retrieving the one-time code there, and feeding it back, they took over accounts — including high-profile handles — with some attackers reportedly also using AI-generated selfie video to defeat identity checks. Meta confirmed roughly 20,225 accounts may have been affected, then disabled the tool, removed the faulty code, invalidated reset links, and forced extra verification. (KrebsOnSecurity · TechCrunch · NYT)

🛡️ Why it matters for SDC — This is the cleanest production example yet of agentic-AI tool-authority risk: the model didn’t need to be “jailbroken,” it was simply given a privileged tool (mutate account recovery state) without a hard identity-binding control behind it. Client talking points: (1) inventory which support/ops agents can perform privileged identity or account actions (email relink, password reset, MFA reset) and require step-up human-in-the-loop or cryptographic identity binding on those actions; (2) treat agent tool permissions as a least-privilege boundary subject to the same review as an API scope, not a UX convenience. REVIEW: the exact 20,225 figure is Meta-attributed via secondary reporting; NYT confirms “tens of thousands.”

Frontier-model availability is now an export-control / continuity risk. Anthropic’s worldwide shutdown of Fable 5 and Mythos 5 — triggered because it could not filter access by nationality in real time — turned a US export directive into a global outage for every customer. (Fortune · Forbes)

🛡️ Why it matters for SDC — Single-vendor frontier dependency now carries a geopolitical continuity risk that didn’t exist a quarter ago: a model your client’s workload depends on can be pulled with hours of notice. Talking points: (1) maintain a multi-model fallback path for any production-critical workload (provider + model abstraction, tested failover); (2) for regulated/multinational clients, the “must restrict by user nationality” requirement is now a concrete control to design for, not a hypothetical. REVIEW: no standalone Anthropic newsroom statement on restoration was located at drafting time; takedown specifics rest on Fortune/Forbes plus secondary explainers — confirm before quoting to clients.
